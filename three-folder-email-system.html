A Simple Three-Folder System for Taming Your Inbox

The Kernel Patch — plain-language tech notes

Most overflowing inboxes aren't a technology problem — they're a decision-making problem. Every email is a tiny unmade decision sitting there, and they pile up. This system gives every email exactly one of three homes, so your inbox stops being a dumping ground and starts being a working space.

Set up three folders under your inbox

1. Processed

You did something with it and don't need to look at it again. This is where finished business goes to rest.

2. To Handle

You've read it, you know you need to do something about it, but it needs thought or another step first. This folder is your primary to-do list — if you use your email to track tasks, this is that list.

3. Artifacts

There's something in it you need to save somewhere — but you haven't decided where yet, or you need to set that place up first. Once it's saved and handled, it moves to Processed. Think of this as your secondary to-do list.

The daily rule

Every email gets one of three treatments, no exceptions:

  1. Delete it or unsubscribe. You don't need it, don't need to act on it, and don't want to see it again.
  2. Respond immediately, then move it to Processed. If it takes two minutes, just do it now.
  3. Move it to To Handle or Artifacts. Whichever fits — then move it to Processed once you've dealt with it.

The goal isn't a permanently empty inbox — it's an inbox where nothing sits unsorted. Once something has a home, it's out of your mental "I should deal with that" list and into an actual, findable folder.
