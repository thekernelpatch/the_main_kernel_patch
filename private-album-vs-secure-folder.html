Private Album vs. Secure Folder on Samsung Phones

The Kernel Patch — plain-language tech notes

If you've got a newer Samsung phone, you've probably seen both of these terms and wondered which one you actually need. Here's the short version.

Private Album is a hidden album built right into the Gallery app. There's no separate password to set — it unlocks with whatever you already use to unlock your phone (fingerprint, PIN, or pattern). Anything you move into it disappears from your regular photo grid, and screenshots are disabled inside it for extra privacy.

Secure Folder is a bigger deal: it's a whole separate, encrypted space on your phone with its own apps, files, and accounts — like a second, locked-down phone living inside your phone. It's built for keeping entire categories of stuff private, not just a handful of pictures, and it's set up separately from your regular lock screen.

For most people, the difference comes down to scope:

  • Want to hide a few sensitive photos (like a BitLocker recovery key, a passport scan, or medical info)? Private Album is the quick, lightweight option.
  • Want an entire locked-down area with its own apps and accounts? That's what Secure Folder is for.

Setting up Private Album

  1. Open Gallery and tap the menu icon (bottom right).
  2. Select Private album and follow the prompt to confirm your lock screen method.
  3. To hide a photo: open it, tap More (⋮), then Move to private album — or long-press to select several at once from the main grid, then use the three-dot menu.
  4. To view it later: open the Gallery menu again and select Private album — it'll ask for your fingerprint or lock screen code.
Don't see "Private album" in your Gallery menu? It's a newer feature (One UI 8.5) still rolling out to devices. If it's missing, check for a Gallery app update first. The "Gallery Labs" manual-enable toggle (Gallery → Settings → About Gallery → tap the version number repeatedly) has worked for some users on older or beta software, but this isn't documented by Samsung directly — treat it as a workaround to try, not a guaranteed step.
